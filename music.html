Manatee · Dreamcast audio & sound driver, explained
The Manatee driver

The MIDI engine & voices

Music, and any sound the game chooses to play as notes, goes through a General-MIDI-like engine: 8 ports of 16 MIDI channels each, sharing 48 hardware voices. Instruments come from tone banks whose samples carry complete AICA register settings.

Tone banks: programs, layers and splits

Everything inside a bank is position-independent: the driver adds the bank's address when it follows offsets, including sample addresses, so the SH-4 can place a bank anywhere in sound RAM. This driver also carries two tiny built-in banks: a default instrument bank (a square and a sawtooth wave) that every channel points at after boot, and a default drum kit built on the noise generator.

What happens on note-on

  1. Find the instrumentThe event names a port, channel, note and velocity. The channel's current program gives up to four layers; muted layers are skipped.
  2. Pick a split per layerThe first split whose key range and velocity range both contain the note wins.
  3. Get a voiceThe allocator returns a free voice or steals one (see below). The voice records who owns it: port, channel, note, velocity and the port's priority.
  4. Program the AICA channelThe split's register image is copied into the channel, with adjustments: sample address relocated by the bank address; envelope and filter-envelope rates offset by the channel's controllers; filter levels offset by the channel's cutoff; LFO depth scaled by the modulation wheel; send bus and send level from the tone, channel and port; pan from the tone or channel plus the port pan; resonance and filter on/off from controllers.
  5. LoudnessThe velocity passes through the bank's velocity curve and is multiplied by the channel level (volume × expression) and the tone's own level; the port volume is added as an offset, and the result becomes the channel's total level.
  6. PitchThe note's distance from the split's root key, the port pitch, the channel's pitch bend and the split's fine tune give a pitch in 1/256 semitones, converted into octave and fraction with two lookup tables.
  7. Key onUnless the layer asks for a delay, the channel is keyed on at once. A delayed layer is keyed on by the 4 ms update when its counter expires, unless a note-off arrives first.

A real bug in this build

If every one of the 48 voices has a higher priority than the new note, the allocator is supposed to fail. Instead, because of a pointer that is only reset inside the loop, it reads two of its own instruction words as "the chosen voice" and reports success, and note-on then writes to garbage addresses. It only happens when all voices are busy with higher-priority sounds. Details on the quirks page.

Note off and the sustain pedal

Note-off releases every voice playing that port, channel and note (duplicates included) by clearing its key-on, so the AICA moves the voice into its release phase. If the sustain pedal (CC 64) was down, the voice is only marked as released and keeps sounding; lifting the pedal releases all such voices on the channel. The voice's "age" restarts and its level is reset, so the allocator will prefer it once it is quiet.

Controllers

Standard MIDI controllers behave as expected, and Sega added a set of controllers that edit the AICA parameters of the tone in real time. Controllers that change something audible also update voices already sounding on that channel.

Pitch bend and program change

Pitch bend is scaled by the bend range (from the layer, or an RPN override) and applied at once to every sounding voice of the channel. Program change looks up the current bank (falling back to the built-in bank), and switches only if the program exists; notes already playing are unaffected. For drums, a host command marks a MIDI channel as a drum channel, whose bank select chooses between two drum-kit bank slots.

What happens every 4 ms

  • Delayed key-ons count down and fire.
  • Port changes (volume, pan and pitch fades driven by host commands) are applied to every voice of the port, so a fade-out of the music affects notes already sounding.
  • Envelope monitoring continues in the background every millisecond, keeping each voice's "how loud am I" value fresh for the allocator.
